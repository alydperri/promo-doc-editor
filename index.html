01 — Philosophy

An editor is not a rewriter

Writing is thinking. The discipline of putting a promotion case into words forces the manager to confront whether the evidence actually supports the decision. When the writing is hard, that difficulty is information.

Most AI tools will identify the problems in your promo doc and then hand you a rewritten version. The problems get fixed but the thinking gets skipped. This editor doesn't rewrite anything. It points at the three lines that don't work, cites the rule they violate, explains why a reviewer would flag them, and hands the document back to you. You do the fixing. That's where the thinking happens.

02 — Example output

What a critique looks like

Here's an excerpt from the editor reviewing a Promotion Assessment section. Every finding cites a rule, quotes the passage, and tells the writer what to fix — without rewriting a word.

Sample output — Promotion Assessment review (excerpt)
Overall assessment

The assessment has strong data in places but reads more like a highlight reel than a promotion argument. Several key claims are unsupported, and the language leans heavily on opinion where evidence should carry the weight.

R5 Objective
"Jim's amazing thought leadership on solving for scale has set an almost unreachable bar. He entirely owned the holistic analytical activity and totally revolutionized how we think about offers."

Exaggerated language throughout: "amazing," "unreachable bar," "entirely owned," "totally revolutionized." These are opinions, not evidence. A promo reviewer reading this will question your judgment, not Jim's performance.

What to fix

Strip the superlatives. For each claim, ask: what specific thing did Jim do, and what was the measurable result? "Jim built the forecasting model for offer cancellations, predicting rates within 3% accuracy across Q1-Q3" is defensible. "Totally revolutionized" is not.

R8 Specific
"He entirely owned the holistic analytical activity."

This names an action but no result. What was the "analytical activity"? What did Jim produce? What changed because of it? A reviewer outside your team has no idea what this means.

What to fix

Complete the example: what was the situation, what did Jim do, and what was the outcome? Use SBI or STAR. If the analytical work led to a decision, name the decision and its impact.

R4 Balanced
"There is no playbook for this business… Jim's amazing thought leadership… totally revolutionized…"

Every claim in this passage is a pure win with no nuance. What went wrong? What did Jim learn? A balanced assessment that acknowledges misses is more credible than a highlights reel.

What to fix

Add the context a reviewer would want: what was hard about this, where did Jim stumble, and how did he recover? One honest miss strengthens the credibility of every win around it.

Notice what's missing? No rewritten version. No "here's how I'd say it." The editor tells you what's broken and why — you decide how to fix it. The full output also includes which sections were reviewed, what's working well, and a prioritized revision order.
03 — Coverage

What it reviews

The editor evaluates promotion documents against criteria organized around four core sections. Your doc doesn't need to use these exact names — the editor maps your content to the relevant criteria regardless of how your organization structures its promotion documents.

Scope of role

Is it about the job, not the person? Is it specific to this role, not a generic description? Does it align to next-level expectations?

Promotion assessment

Is it balanced, objective, criteria-focused, and specific? Does it stand on its own without insider knowledge?

Growth areas

Are they present, honest, and specific? Is there a remediation plan? Has feedback been incorporated?

Feedback

Both reasons to promote and reasons not to promote? A clear vote? Performance examples, not personality descriptions?

04 — The rubric

Every finding cites a rule

23 rules

No finding without a citation. No vague "consider improving this." The writer can trace every piece of feedback back to a specific standard.

Scope of role

  • R1 About the job, not the person
  • R2 Specific, not generic
  • R3 Criteria-aligned

Promotion assessment

  • R4 Balanced
  • R5 Objective
  • R6 Values-aligned
  • R7 Criteria-focused
  • R8 Specific
  • R9 Stands on its own
  • R10 Clear and concise

Growth areas

  • R11 No missing growth areas
  • R12 Objective
  • R13 Criteria-focused
  • R14 Specific
  • R15 Gaps being closed
  • R16 Incorporates feedback

Feedback

  • R17 Balanced feedback
  • R18 Clear vote

Writing quality — all sections

  • W1 Clarity above all
  • W2 Active voice
  • W3 No jargon without context
  • W4 Cut what doesn't serve
  • W5 No format over substance
